Talk to Sev / Content Workshop

Before we
get into it.

A short brief to bring me up to speed on you, your business, and how you actually think. Record your answers as voice memos. Talk like you're explaining it to a smart friend, not writing a report.

Pre-Workshop

The surface level. You and your business in plain English.

Don't overthink these. One voice memo, top to bottom, off the cuff. Aim for 10 to 15 minutes total. We'll get into the specifics inside the workshop.

  1. 01
    Introduce yourself. Your name, where you're based, what you do in plain English.
  2. 02
    Walk me through your backstory. How did you end up doing what you do today?
  3. 03
    What did you do before this work, and what made you pivot?
  4. 04
    In simple terms, who do you help and what problem do you solve for them?
  5. 05
    Why this work? What keeps you in it on the hard days?
  6. 06
    What do you want to be known for in your industry?
  7. 07
    What does winning look like for you over the next 12 months?
  8. 08
    What's something most people get wrong about you or your business?
  9. 09
    How would you describe what you do to a 10 year old?
  10. 10
    Tell me about a recent moment that reminded you why you do this.
Pro tip One continuous recording beats ten clean ones. The pauses and tangents are where the good stuff lives. If you fumble a sentence, just keep going and restate it. We can edit later.
Going Deeper

The specifics that make the content sound like you, not like AI.

This is the meat. The stuff AI can't make up: real customer language, contrarian beliefs, specific stories, pricing reality. Tackle it as one long memo, or one per category. Whatever flows.

A / The Customer in Their Own Words

How they describe the problem before they meet you.

  1. 01
    Read out 3 to 5 verbatim DMs, emails, or things customers have said in calls about their problem. Don't clean them up.
  2. 02
    What words do they actually use for the problem? Not industry jargon, their language.
  3. 03
    What did they try before you that didn't work, and why didn't it work?
  4. 04
    What's the emotional cost of the problem, not just the financial one?
  5. 05
    What's the one objection that kills more deals than any other?
B / Voice and POV

The contrarian gold. Where you disagree with your industry.

  1. 06
    What's the loudest piece of advice in your industry that you think is wrong, and why?
  2. 07
    What does your industry get away with that pisses you off?
  3. 08
    If you had 60 seconds on stage in front of every potential client, what would you say?
  4. 09
    Who's the customer you actively don't want, and why?
  5. 10
    What's a belief you hold that costs you clients but you refuse to drop?
C / The Big 5

The questions every buyer asks before they buy.

  1. 11
    Pricing: what does this actually cost, what drives the price up or down, and when is it not worth it?
  2. 12
    Problems: what are the legitimate downsides, risks, or failure modes of what you sell?
  3. 13
    Comparisons: how do you stack up against the 3 main alternatives, including doing nothing?
  4. 14
    Reviews: what do customers say in their own words, good and bad?
  5. 15
    Best in class: what makes a great version of what you do, versus an average one?
D / Stories and Proof

The moments and transformations that prove you can.

  1. 16
    Founder origin. Why this business, why now, what's the scar tissue behind it?
  2. 17
    Three specific customer transformations with numbers, timelines, and names if you can use them.
  3. 18
    The biggest failure or near miss in the business, and what changed because of it.
  4. 19
    A moment a customer said something that made you realise you were onto something.
E / Competitive and Cultural Context

The world your customer lives in.

  1. 20
    Three competitors or category adjacent players you respect, and one thing each does well.
  2. 21
    Three accounts, podcasts, or newsletters your ideal customer follows.
  3. 22
    What's happening in the industry right now that nobody's talking about?
F / Business Specifics and Execution

The practical stuff that shapes what we make.

  1. 23
    What do you sell, at what price, and how do they pay? One off, retainer, or package?
  2. 24
    What's your average customer worth over 12 months?
  3. 25
    What content has converted customers for you (yours or anyone else's), and why do you think it worked?
  4. 26
    What have you tried that flopped?
  5. 27
    What formats can you realistically produce each week. Video, audio, written, carousel?
  6. 28
    What's the one piece of content you've been meaning to make for 6 months and haven't?
The bonus take Record a 5 minute unscripted memo answering: "Explain what you do and who you do it for, like I'm a smart friend at a barbecue." Off the cuff, no script. That single file is worth more than any structured answer for capturing how you actually talk.
The How

Recording voice memos. Three platforms, no excuses.

You don't need fancy gear. The phone in your pocket is fine. Find a quiet room, hold it close to your face, and talk like you're on a call to a mate.

iPhone

Voice Memos

Pre-installed. White icon with a red soundwave.
  1. Open the Voice Memos app. Can't find it? Swipe down on the home screen and search voice memos.
  2. Tap the big red circle at the bottom of the screen to start recording.
  3. Talk. Tap the red square to stop when you're done.
  4. Tap the recording, then tap the title (it'll say "New Recording") to rename it. Something like Shallow End - [Your Name].
  5. To send: tap the three dots next to the recording, then Share. Pick email, AirDrop, or Messages.
Android

Recorder or Voice Recorder

Pre-installed on Pixel and Samsung. Otherwise grab Google Recorder from the Play Store (free).
  1. Open the Recorder app (Pixel), Voice Recorder (Samsung), or download Google Recorder from the Play Store.
  2. Tap the red microphone button to start recording.
  3. Talk. Tap stop when you're done.
  4. Tap the recording to rename it. Use something like Shallow End - [Your Name].
  5. To send: tap the share icon on the recording. Pick email, Drive, or whatever's handy.
Mac

QuickTime Player

Pre-installed. In your Applications folder, or hit Cmd + Space and search "QuickTime".
  1. Open QuickTime Player.
  2. In the menu bar: File then New Audio Recording.
  3. Click the red circle to start. Click it again to stop.
  4. Save with Cmd + S. Give it a clear name and pick a folder you can find again.
  5. Email it as an attachment, or upload to Google Drive and share the link.
Windows

Sound Recorder

Pre-installed. Search "Sound Recorder" or "Voice Recorder" in the Start menu.
  1. Click Start and search Sound Recorder or Voice Recorder.
  2. Click the red microphone to start recording.
  3. Click stop when you're done. Files save automatically.
  4. Right click the recording and select Show in folder to locate the file.
  5. Attach to an email, or upload to Google Drive and share the link.
Recording tips Hold the phone like you're on a call, close to your mouth. Find a quiet room with soft furnishings (a bedroom beats a kitchen). Don't worry about ums and ahs. One take beats a perfect script. The longer you ramble, the more raw material we get to work with.
